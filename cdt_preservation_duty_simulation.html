Electronic Discovery — Duty to Preserve: Case Study (Consumer / Product Claims)
When does the duty to preserve trigger?
Circuit Direct, Inc. ("CDT") — Consumer Class Action Scenario
The legal standard

The duty to preserve arises when litigation is reasonably anticipated — that is, when a party knows or reasonably should know that evidence may be relevant to future litigation.

This is an objective standard: not when suit is filed, not when a lawyer is retained, but when a reasonable person in the party's position would foresee the prospect of litigation.

Background

The company: Circuit Direct, Inc. ("CDT"), a Miami-based online reseller of computer components (~$10 million in 2015 revenue), founded in 2013 by siblings Jane Fioro (President) and John Fioro (COO). Neither has ever taken a salary; both own all of the company's stock. John is a member of the Florida Bar and practiced litigation before co-founding CDT.

The pressure point: On August 5, 2016, investors conditioned a $100 million investment on CDT reaching profitability for calendar year 2016. CDT responded with an aggressive "Profitability in 2016" campaign — including a "We Guarantee... Double Your Money Back in Ten Days" promise and a 25% salary increase for employees if the company turns a profit. The campaign supercharges sales of the SuperDrive, a top-selling external hard drive sourced from a Chinese supplier, DrivesPlus, whose products have carried return rates as high as 50%.
