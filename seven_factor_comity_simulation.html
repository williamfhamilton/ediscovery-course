Electronic Discovery — International Cross-Border Discovery
Seven factors, one ruling
Palmetto Ridge Europe SAS — Plaintiff’s Motion to Compel the Lyon Materials
The legal standard

When a foreign law stands in the way of US discovery, the Hague Evidence Convention is not the exclusive route. In Société Nationale Industrielle Aérospatiale, 482 U.S. 522 (1987), the Supreme Court sent courts to a comity analysis and pointed to the Restatement factors. Lower courts commonly add two more. This exercise uses seven:

  1. The importance to the litigation of the documents requested
  2. The degree of specificity of the request
  3. Whether the information originated in the United States
  4. The availability of alternative means of securing the information
  5. The balance of national interests: the interests of the United States against the interests of the state where the information sits
  6. The hardship of compliance on the party resisting discovery
  7. The good faith of the party resisting discovery

The factors do not come with weights or a formula. Counting them is not the same as weighing them. That is the judgment you are here to practice.

Your role and the record

You are the magistrate judge. Darnell Washington’s race discrimination case is pending in the Middle District of Florida. Plaintiff served Request No. 4 on January 12 seeking all communications about promotion decisions company-wide, including the Lyon subsidiary, Palmetto Ridge Europe SAS. Palmetto Ridge produced its Tampa documents and objected to the Lyon materials on French-law grounds, but first raised that objection in its opposition to the motion to compel, seven months after the request. The discovery cutoff is four months away.

The material at stake: the mailbox of Camille Laurent, a Lyon operations supervisor, which holds her replies to Operations Manager Kyle Reeves about how the “culture fit” standard was applied to lead roles. Reeves’s own messages in the thread have been produced from Tampa. Camille’s replies exist only in Lyon.

Your task: for each factor, decide which way it points: toward compelling production, toward protecting the data, or neutral. Then rule on the motion.
