Lost text messages and Rule 37(e)
Bayshore Provisions v. Tidewater Pallet Co., et al. — Price-Fixing Scenario
The legal standard — Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 37(e)

Rule 37(e) governs the loss of electronically stored information (ESI) that should have been preserved for litigation. Before the court may act at all, four things must be true:

  1. The ESI should have been preserved in anticipation or conduct of litigation.
  2. The ESI is lost.
  3. It was lost because a party failed to take reasonable steps to preserve it.
  4. It cannot be restored or replaced through additional discovery.

If all four are met, the rule offers two tiers of response. Under (e)(1), on a finding of prejudice to another party, the court may order measures no greater than necessary to cure the prejudice. Under (e)(2), only on a finding that the party acted with the intent to deprive another party of the information’s use in the litigation, the court may presume the lost information was unfavorable, instruct the jury that it may or must make that presumption, or dismiss the action or enter default judgment.

Negligence, even gross negligence, will not support the harshest sanctions. Intent is the line.

Background

The case: A putative class of direct purchasers, led by Bayshore Provisions, a Gulf Coast food distributor, sues three Southeast wooden-pallet manufacturers under Section 1 of the Sherman Act. The complaint alleges they agreed to fix pallet prices over roughly two years. The case is pending in the Middle District of Florida.

Your client: Tidewater Pallet Co., a Jacksonville manufacturer with about 450 employees. The co-defendants are Gulf Coast Pallet and Southern Crate.

The people: Gary Pruitt, Tidewater’s VP of Sales, who uses his own iPhone for work under a bring-your-own-device policy. Lena Marsh, Tidewater’s CEO, who has a company-issued phone. Anita Voss, Tidewater’s general counsel. Hal Brennan, Gulf Coast’s head of sales and Pruitt’s longtime industry contact.

Each defendant keeps its own texts on its own phones, so no single custodian holds the whole story.
